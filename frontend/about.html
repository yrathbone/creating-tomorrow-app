Why Creating Tomorrow Exists

Opportunity shouldn't depend on knowing the secret language.

Creating Tomorrow exists to make career knowledge, tools, and education easier to understand — and free to use.

Real skills. Clear guidance. Brighter tomorrows.

Most People Have More to Offer Than They Know How to Explain

A job title rarely tells the whole story.

Someone may have spent years solving problems, helping customers, training coworkers, organizing projects, learning technology, managing money, caring for people, or keeping an operation running — and still struggle when someone asks:

“What are your skills?”

That gap matters.

Career systems often assume people already know how resumes work, what an ATS is, how to prepare for interviews, how to describe transferable skills, or where to go when they need to learn something new.

Many people were simply never taught.

Creating Tomorrow was built to help change that.

Not by telling people what to pretend they know.

By helping them recognize what they actually know, describe it clearly, identify what they still need to learn, and take the next step.

That's the idea.

A Note From Jo

“I didn't build Creating Tomorrow because I think everyone needs a better resume. I built it because I think far too many people underestimate what they already know.”

I started my career in banking in an entry-level role. Over time, that career grew into work involving treasury, payments, technology, client consulting, complex implementations, and large organizations.

There wasn't one magical moment where somebody handed me a map and said, “Here is how careers work.”

Much of it came from learning one thing, trying something new, asking questions, making mistakes, building another skill, and eventually realizing that experience compounds.

And somewhere along the way, I began helping other people with their resumes.

What struck me wasn't how little experience people had.

It was how much experience they couldn't see in themselves.

Someone might say:

“I just work in customer service.”

Then we talk.

They trained new employees.

They handled escalations.

They balanced competing priorities.

They learned multiple systems.

They solved problems nobody else wanted.

They communicated with people who were frustrated, confused, or scared.

Those aren't “just” duties.

Those are skills.

And once people learn to recognize them, something changes.

Not because we invented anything.

Because we finally gave the experience a name.

People often have the experience before they have the words.

Honest Guidance Matters

I don't want Creating Tomorrow to tell people they're qualified for everything.

Sometimes a job really is a stretch.

Sometimes there is a skill someone doesn't have yet.

Sometimes the answer is:

“Not yet.”

That's okay.

I'd rather give someone an honest gap and show them where they can learn than give them a flattering score that sends them into an interview unprepared.

Creating Tomorrow is not here to manufacture confidence.

It's here to help build confidence on something stronger:

Evidence.

Career Education Shouldn't Be Another Thing Hidden Behind a Paywall

There are extraordinary educational resources in the world.

There are also millions of people trying to improve their lives while navigating work, family, money, school, caregiving, transportation, and everything else adulthood decides to throw at them.

Career information should not require an expensive resume writer.

Understanding an ATS should not require becoming an HR expert.

Learning how to interview should not require buying a course.

Discovering that you have transferable skills should not require somebody else recognizing them first.

Why “Creating Tomorrow”?

Tomorrow isn't something we arrive at all at once.

We create it in remarkably ordinary ways.

A course.

A conversation.

A book.

An application.

A new skill.

A question we were finally willing to ask.

A resume we were afraid to send.

An interview we almost talked ourselves out of.

One decision rarely changes an entire life.

But decisions accumulate. Skills accumulate. Knowledge accumulates. Confidence accumulates.

And eventually, the tomorrow that once looked very far away starts looking a little more possible.

Creating Tomorrow is about helping people take that next step.

Not someone else's next step.

Theirs.

What research shows. What credible guidance recommends. What Creating Tomorrow suggests as a practical habit.

Technology Should Help People See More Possibility — Not Less

AI can analyze language remarkably well.

But its purpose here is not to decide someone's value.

Creating Tomorrow uses technology to help people:

  • identify patterns
  • recognize skills
  • compare experience with opportunities
  • improve clarity
  • find learning paths
  • understand information that might otherwise feel inaccessible

The person remains the source of truth.

If the experience isn't theirs, it doesn't belong on their resume.

About Jo

Jo Rathbone is a banking, treasury, payments, and financial-technology professional whose career grew from frontline banking into complex enterprise consulting and technology-enabled financial solutions.

Her work across client service, banking operations, treasury, payments, and technology shaped a recurring observation: people frequently possess valuable skills long before they learn the professional language used to describe them.

Creating Tomorrow grew from years of helping others recognize that experience, build stronger career materials, and see possibilities they had overlooked.

She believes technology is most useful when it expands human potential rather than replacing human judgment.
